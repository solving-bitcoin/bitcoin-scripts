# Correlations do not remove the quadratic subset-label boundary

Question: can correlated candidate scalars extend the DH quartet into the
full 5-of-54 alphabet, preserving one of two fixed hidden point labels for
each output bit?

**Not within the quadratic interface below.** The missing-three boundary
also holds for correlated linear candidates, provided every unselected scalar
must remain unavailable after any allowed t-subset opens. The key argument
is a polynomial degree bound, rather than independence of all candidates.
For 5-of-54, the resulting necessary degree sum is 50, whereas two quadratic
labels give only four. This rules out a particular route from the new native
quartet wrapper to the compact subset layout; it is not a general pointlock
impossibility claim.

## Model

Let `x` be a vector of hidden scalars over the secp256k1 order field, and let
candidate `i` be the public linear form `ell_i(x)`. Public setup includes
the coefficient points `x_j*G` and the derived candidate points. All t-subsets
of candidates are allowed. Opening S reveals its indexed scalar values.

Each output label is a fixed vector of quadratic polynomial **point values**
`q(x)*G`, with publicly specified coefficients. It is not the scalar q(x).
Public metadata supplies no additional secret-derived quadratic points.
The coefficient and polynomial descriptions are fixed independently of the
hidden coefficient vector. Secret-dependent descriptions that supply further
nonlinear constraints are outside this model.
Evaluation uses group operations with known coefficients, including coefficients
computed from the opened scalars. Every selection must reconstruct an entire
label; its opposite must retain a nonpublic quadratic component. This is an
algebraic reconstruction model, not an information-theoretic claim about
public curve points or a proof about every nonlinear evaluation algorithm.

The requirement that no t-subset recover an unselected candidate implies that
**every t+1 candidate forms are independent**. Otherwise a minimal dependency
expresses one form using at most t others; extending those others to a full
t-subset exposes an additional scalar. Correlations involving larger sets
remain allowed. For example, N evaluations of a secret degree-t polynomial
use only t+1 independent coefficients and satisfy this premise.

This premise has a deliberate scope. A different code may restrict allowed
subsets or permit some extra scalar disclosures while binding all such views
to the same message. Such an interface needs its own native checks and label
argument; it is not included in this full-inventory theorem.

## What one opening permits

Write a fiber of the opened equations as `x=u+Kz`. For a quadratic component,

```text
q(u+Kz) = constant + linear(z) + homogeneous_quadratic(z).
```

The first two terms can be evaluated as points from the opened values and
public linear points. A remaining nonzero quadratic term is outside the
specified reconstruction interface. Therefore availability requires the
homogeneous quadratic part q2 to vanish identically on `ker(ell_i : i in S)`.
Equivalently, as a formal polynomial,

```text
q2 belongs to I_S = (ell_i : i in S).
```

Choose one nonzero quadratic component `q0` from the first nonpublic label
and one `q1` from its opposite. Vector width does not help: reconstructing
an entire label requires that particular component too. If every t-subset
reconstructs at least one of the labels, then

```text
q0*q1 belongs to the intersection of I_S over every t-subset S.
```

The product is nonzero and has degree four. Requiring exactly one label is
stronger; the obstruction already applies to total reconstruction of at
least one of two nonpublic labels.

## The degree lemma

For N linear forms with every t+1 independent, every nonzero homogeneous
polynomial in that intersection has degree at least

```text
N-t+1.
```

An elementary proof works directly with polynomial ideals. For t=1, the
polynomial is divisible by every distinct linear form, so has degree at least
N. For t>1, suppose f had smaller degree. Restrict it to `ell_i=0`. The other
forms, restricted to this hyperplane, have every t independent; the restriction
belongs to every ideal generated by t-1 of them. Induction gives the same
minimum degree `(N-1)-(t-1)+1=N-t+1`, so the restriction must be zero. Thus
`ell_i` divides f. This holds for every i. Their product divides f, which
contradicts `degree(f)<N-t+1<=N`.

The bound is attained by the product of any N-t+1 candidate forms: every
t-subset meets that product's factor set. This establishes the minimum degree,
not a complete label scheme at that degree.

This is the initial-degree property of star-configuration ideals, related
to Proposition 2.9(4) of Geramita, Harbourne and Migliore,
[Star configurations in P^n, arXiv:1203.5685v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1203.5685v1).
The reference treats the algebraic-geometric setting; the direct ideal
argument above supplies the field-algebra statement used here. In finite
fields, membership of a formal polynomial ideal must not be replaced by
vanishing of an arbitrary polynomial function on sampled points.

Combining the lemma with `degree(q0*q1)=4` gives

```text
N-t <= 3.
```

In particular, correlated full 5-of-54 candidates cannot supply this
quadratic two-label interface. More generally, the same necessary argument
for two nonzero leading forms of degrees d0,d1 gives `d0+d1 >= N-t+1`,
whenever their reconstruction requires those leading forms to vanish on the
unopened fiber. At 5-of-54, at least one of these degrees must be 25 or more.
This is not a construction with degree-25 labels: vanishing of a leading
term alone does not make all lower unknown terms evaluable, and any extra
nonlinear public metadata needs its own binding and setup accounting.

## The correlated positive boundary

The degree obstruction does not remove the quartet or all correlated DH
schemes. Set N=t+3 and let candidate i be evaluation at i+1 of a hidden
degree-t polynomial. Public candidate points follow by ordinary linear
combinations of its t+1 coefficient points.

Partition candidates into two groups, both of size at least two. Each label
is the vector of DH products for every pair within its group. Exactly three
candidates are missing. Exactly one group contains at least two missing
candidates. Its label has an unavailable DH component; the other group's
entire label can be computed by multiplying a public candidate point by a
selected endpoint's scalar.

There is also a direct one-dimensional fiber evaluation. Set the last secret
coefficient to z and solve the opened values for `x=u+h*z`. The direction h
is the coefficient vector of `product(v-(i+1) : i in S)`. For a pair i,j,

```text
ell_i(x)*ell_j(x) = a_i*a_j
                       + (a_i*b_j+a_j*b_i)*z
                       + b_i*b_j*z^2.
```

Any unopened candidate has nonzero b_i by the independence premise. A pair
with a selected endpoint has zero quadratic coefficient and is reconstructed
from the public point zG. An unavailable pair has `b_i*b_j != 0`; an oracle
giving its point yields z^2G after subtracting the known constant and linear
points and dividing by that coefficient. This is a square-CDH embedding for
honest random coefficient secrets, with deterministic curve controls here.

The positive boundary supplies one binary vector-label classifier. It does
not by itself give all message bits, a composed verifier, public certification
of arbitrary creator-chosen entropy, or a native pointlock publication.
